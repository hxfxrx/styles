# Dutch A2 Study Module — Nederlands voor beginners

A self-study course that takes you from zero (or scattered basics) to **CEFR level A2** in Dutch
over **12 weeks**, at roughly **6–8 hours of study per week** (~90 hours total, which matches the
commonly cited 80–120 guided learning hours for A1 → A2).

## What A2 means (CEFR "Waystage")

By the end of this course you should be able to:

- Understand sentences and frequent expressions about everyday topics (personal information,
  family, shopping, work, your local area).
- Handle simple, routine exchanges: ordering food, asking directions, making appointments,
  small talk about the weather and your weekend.
- Describe in simple terms your background, daily routine, and immediate needs.
- Read short everyday texts: menus, timetables, simple emails, signs, short news items.
- Write short notes, messages, and a simple personal letter or email.

## How to use this course

Each module covers two weeks and contains:

1. **Vocabulary** — themed word lists. Put these into a spaced-repetition app (Anki, Quizlet)
   the day you start the module and review daily (15 min/day minimum).
2. **Grammar** — short explanations with examples. Read once, then learn through the exercises.
3. **Dialogues & texts** — read aloud, twice. Speaking the words matters more than understanding
   every detail.
4. **Exercises** — with an answer key at the bottom of each module. Do them on paper or in a
   note file *before* checking the answers.
5. **Speaking & writing tasks** — the part most self-learners skip and the part that actually
   gets you to A2. Record yourself on your phone; write the writing tasks out in full.

### Suggested weekly rhythm (≈7 hours)

| Day | Activity | Time |

## Ground rules for success

- **Daily beats long.** 30 minutes every day outperforms 4 hours on Sunday.
- **Speak from day one**, even alone. Dutch pronunciation (the *g*, *ui*, *eu*) needs muscle memory.
- **Don't translate word-for-word.** Dutch word order will fight you; learn whole phrases.
- **Tolerate ambiguity.** At A2 you will not understand everything. That's normal and fine.
